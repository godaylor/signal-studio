# M22 design contract: a product question with visible evidence

Subject: a product team investigating the signup → setup → useful feature → subscription path. First-time visitors must understand what the product does, what the visible example means, and what they can change. Guest Orbit is an explicitly synthetic software product dataset, not live customer traffic.

Keep Kinorit identity. Palette: midnight #061b2c, water #35e0cf, cobalt #5089ff, warm loss #ffb381, paper #f0f6f8, ink #0e293d. Existing OFL Golos Text for UI, Unbounded for small brand accents, tabular data numerals. No new fonts or dependencies.

Signature: a measurable journey. Gates represent ordered actions; stream thickness represents participant count relative to the first stage; warm branches represent adjacent drop-off. Increase visual legibility and depth, but explain only things computed from the actual response. No invented reasons, recommendations masquerading as findings, or generated UI screenshots presented as implementation.

Composition: question → concise plain-language answer → visual evidence → optional detailed table/definitions. Suggested guest questions change the normal normalized URL query; no tour or blocked onboarding. The query spine remains the visible editable definition. On narrow devices, compact summary stays readable and dense charts/tables get named local scrolling. Wide displays keep readable content columns instead of stretching controls to 8K.

Critique of M21: generated concept had richer luminous paths and explanatory hierarchy than the working scene. Generic activation/value labels obscured what happened. Large empty areas and tiny captions amplified the gap. M22 must judge success from actual screenshots and working scenarios; concept images are historical inspiration, not pixel-accurate acceptance evidence.
